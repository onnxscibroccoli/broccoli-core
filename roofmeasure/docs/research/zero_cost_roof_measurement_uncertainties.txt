Resolving Technical Uncertainties in
Zero-Cost Residential Roof
Measurement
Producing estimator-grade residential roof reports at zero end-user cost is technically viable for
primary facet areas, dominant pitches, and main ridge bearings wherever public airborne lidar
(USGS 3DEP Quality Level 2 or better) overlaps high-resolution leaf-off state or local
orthoimagery at 15 cm resolution. Where either input is absent—particularly in jurisdictions
restricted to 0.3–0.6 m summer NAIP imagery or lacking lidar coverage—automated pipelines
cannot reliably separate gable ends from hip ends, measure roof overhangs, recover dormer or
skylight curb heights within ±0.5 m, or isolate small outbuildings from terrain clutter without
human verification. An estimator-grade zero-cost system must therefore enforce a strict
provenance boundary: 3-D lidar plane intersections and user-verified geometry drive billable
takeoff totals, while unverified 2-D geometric extrapolations and computer-vision feature
detections remain segregated from material quantities.
Across all 14 technical uncertainties, open datasets introduce systematic biases that break
naive CAD-style assumptions. Public building footprints (Overture Maps, Microsoft, FEMA) trace
outer roof edges rather than exterior walls in imagery-derived areas, while a vendor benchmark
by EarthDefine reports that these datasets omit 19–33% of U.S. structures (concentrated
among garages and sheds under 100 m²). Meanwhile, terrain-rectified 15 cm orthophotos
routinely display 2–6 m of radial building lean that must never be interpreted as overhang.
Lightweight Apache-2.0 neural models—specifically MobileSAM, SAM 2.1 Hiera-Tiny, and Depth
Anything V2 Small—execute completely offline in browsers and on mobile devices, yet serve
reliably only as guided mask refiners or relative-relief cues rather than standalone metric 3-D
reconstructors.

Zero-Cost Data Sources, Endpoints, and Coverage
Gaps
Zero-cost constraints eliminate commercial aerial providers, paid parcel aggregators, and
key-gated APIs whose terms bar multi-user redistribution or reserve high-resolution layers for
paid subscribers. AWS Requester Pays buckets (s3://usgs-lidar/ raw LAZ and
s3://naip-visualization/, naip-analytic/, and naip-source/) bill egress transfer to the caller, and

reported that USDA permanently retired its Geospatial Data Gateway on March 31, 2026,

1. Eave vs. Rake Classification from 2-D Outlines
Roof eaves are horizontal lower perimeter edges that carry gutters and ice barriers, whereas
rakes are sloped gable-end edges climbing from eave to ridge without gutters. When only a 2-D
roofprint and a dominant ridge bearing are known, the most reliable geometric rule combines
ridge-line side testing with vector alignment relative to the ridge unit vector \hat{r}: edges
parallel to \hat{r} (minimum |\hat{e} \times \hat{r}|, e.g., within ±10–15°) whose vertices remain
on one side of the ridge axis are labeled candidate eaves, while edges crossing or running
orthogonal to the ridge line are labeled candidate rakes. Using a cross-product side-of-ridge-line
test outperforms simple distance or aspect-ratio thresholds on near-square footprints.
Nevertheless, 2-D outlines cannot verify whether an orthogonal end edge terminates in a
vertical gable wall or a sloped hip facet: in a study of LiDAR-free LoD1.3-to-LoD2.2
reconstruction across 41 Dutch 3DBAG tiles restricted to rectangular flat, gabled, and hipped
buildings, classification reached only 75.31% under cross-validation and 68.10% on held-out
tiles, identifying gable-versus-hip confusion as the dominant failure ceiling even when building
heights and neighbor context were supplied.
Single-ridge, per-wing, and aspect-based heuristics exhibit distinct failure profiles across roof
shapes. A single-ridge rule accurately labels two eaves and four half-span rakes on pure
rectangular gables, but fails completely on pure hip roofs—which have eaves on all four sides
and zero rakes—causing a 2:1 rectangular hip to lose one-third of its gutter footage and acquire
four phantom rakes. A per-wing decomposition splits L-, T-, U-, and H-shaped footprints into
rectangular sections assuming each wing's ridge runs along its local major axis, resolving
perpendicular cross-gables that a global bearing inverts; however, it still misclassifies square
wings, front-facing cross-gables running along the minor axis, clipped-gable (jerkinhead) ends,
and Dutch gables. A pure 2-D aspect-based method built solely from outward polygon normals
collapses into an unweighted straight-skeleton hip roof (all eaves, no rakes). True aspect
classification requires an elevation gradient \nabla z (lidar or stereo DSM), where eaves satisfy
\nabla z \parallel \hat{n}_e (gradient perpendicular to the level edge) and rakes satisfy \nabla z
\perp \hat{n}_e (gradient parallel to the sloped edge), solving mixed gable/hip roofs reliably.
On L-shaped and multi-ridge houses, the most frequent failure modes occur at re-entrant
corners, attached-garage step-backs, and short facade recesses, where an edge parallel to a
ridge actually abuts vertical siding (requiring apron or step flashing rather than a free-draining
eave) or where flying/prow gables extend the ridge beyond the eave line.
Recommended Low-Confidence Default: When elevation gradients or visual/user gable
confirmations are unavailable, label perimeter edges Unclassified Perimeter (Eave/Rake
Unverified), report total perimeter linear footage without splitting gutter LF from rake trim, or
render a provisional hip-default outline locked until the user toggles confirmed gable ends.

2. Pitch and Ridge Extraction from Public Lidar
Because USGS 3DEP minimum specifications do not require building classification (Class 6),
extraction pipelines must isolate non-ground returns (Class != 2, excluding noise Classes 7 and
18) inside a buffered footprint and strip canopy returns via height consistency and multi-return
filtering. Classical Hough Transform plane extraction underperforms Random Sample
Consensus (RANSAC) in both computational efficiency and segmentation accuracy, while
standard unweighted RANSAC routinely generates spurious cross-ridge planes bridging shallow
adjacent facets.
Two zero-cost, offline geometric algorithms deliver the highest confidence on residential point
clouds: footprint-aligned Weighted RANSAC / MLESAC refined with orthogonal-distance
Principal Component Analysis (PCA), and octree/voxel region growing with hierarchical
clustering and boundary relabeling. Constraining RANSAC hypothesis normals to be
orthogonal (or at 45° hip angles) to dominant footprint vectors suppresses off-axis tilt artifacts on
noisy roofs, while distance- and normal-weighted consensus scoring (Weighted
RANSAC/MLESAC) eliminates bridging planes. Note that at very dense spacing (e.g., 40
pts/m²), shingle, tile, or corrugated-metal micro-relief and ranging jitter make tight-neighborhood
PCA normals noisy; resampling seed neighborhoods to 0.5 m spacing (~4 pts/m²) stabilizes
coplanar seed selection while preserving small 1 m² facets. Ridges, hips, and valleys should be
computed analytically as 3-D plane–plane intersection lines trimmed to adjacent inlier hulls
rather than extracted from gridded raster crests.
No published benchmark in the reviewed literature directly quantifies empirical suburban pitch
RMSE across 1.0 m, 0.5 m, and denser 3DEP clouds against field measurements. However,
USGS 3DEP smooth-surface repeatability bounds (RMSDz ≤ 0.12 m for QL3, ≤0.08 m for QL2,
≤0.03 m intraswath for QL1/QL0) and industry PCA minimums (≥10 points per cell, ≥4 pts/m²
practical density) enable direct Monte Carlo error propagation. Simulating 2,000 ordinary
least-squares plane fits on a 6/12 (26.57°) slope under idealized single-swath Gaussian vertical
noise (\sigma_z) aligned to 3DEP repeatability (excluding tree occlusion and interswath offsets)
defines the theoretical precision floor below:

Real-world failure modes center on interswath calibration differences (up to 0.08 m RMSDz in
QL1/QL2), which can split a single flat plane into two parallel overlapping planes, as well as
dense overhanging canopy creating steep false vegetation facets and legacy tiles dipping below
2 pts/m². To prevent multi-plane fragmentation from inflating linear footage, merge
near-coplanar segments (<5° normal difference and <0.10 m step), enforce non-overlapping 2-D
partition ownership across the roofprint, and demote unmerged or weak hypotheses (<10–15
points or RMSE >0.08 m) to an appendix excluded from linear totals.
Recommended Low-Confidence Default: Exclude unverified plane-intersection segments
from Ridge, Hip, and Valley totals; assign any under-sampled facet (<10 points or RMSE >0.12
m) the structure's area-weighted median verified pitch tagged Pitch Estimated — Low Lidar
Support.

3. Overhang Estimation from Ortho vs. Roofprint
Mismatch
Apparent 2–6 m offsets between lean-corrected roofprints and 15 cm orthophotos reflect radial
building lean and orthorectification error, not roof overhangs. Because standard orthos are
projected onto a bare-earth DTM rather than a DSM, elevated eaves shift radially outward by
\Delta r = h \tan\theta relative to their true planimetric position. Modeling this geometry confirms
why multi-meter offsets are common: a 7 m tall two-story house imaged at 20°–30° off-nadir
shifts 2.55–4.04 m, and a 10 m tall roof at 25°–30° shifts 4.66–5.77 m—before accounting for
NAIP's 6 m (95%) horizontal contract tolerance. By contrast, roofing industry guides place
typical suburban overhangs at 12–24 in (0.30–0.61 m) on eaves and 6–16 in (0.15–0.41 m) on
rakes. Furthermore, imagery-derived footprints (Microsoft, Overture ML fills, EarthDefine, and
typical aerial traces) already capture the outer fascia edge including overhangs, whereas
tax-assessor records and municipal cadastres often describe ground walls. On a 56 × 28 ft
(1,568 sq ft) ranch at 5/12 pitch, adding 18 in eaves and 12 in rakes increases plan area by 230
sq ft (+14.7%, or 249 sq ft / 2.5 squares sloped) and adds 10.5 ft of drip edge—highlighting why
mistaking a 3 m lean offset for overhang would severely distort a bid.
Automated zero-cost methods cannot reliably extract true overhang depth from
ortho-versus-roofprint mismatch alone:
   1.​ Roof-and-facade semantic segmentation: Dual segmentation of roof and exposed
       siding (demonstrated by PMC8749658) recovers the lean vector and wall base along the
       camera-facing elevation, yet leaves the far wall occluded beneath the roof, preventing
       full-perimeter overhang estimation.
   2.​ Multi-date / multi-angle imagery: Comparing two public ortho vintages (e.g., MassGIS
       2021 vs. 2025 or NAIP vs. state imagery) reveals opposing roof-lean directions over
       invariant ground curbs. While this verifies lean direction and stabilizes roof registration,
       both orthos trace the outer roof edge rather than the hidden interior wall plate.
   3.​ Under-eave shadow and fascia cues: Under-eave shadow bands on sunlit walls and
       fascia tone shifts mark the outer shingle stop on crisp 15 cm orthos, but vanish near nadir,
       conflate with projecting gutter lips, and require manual interpretation.
   4.​ Assessor wall footprint vs. QL1 lidar differencing: Actual overhang can be
       approximated automatically only when an explicitly wall-only municipal footprint is paired
       with dense QL1 lidar (≤0.35 m spacing); standard QL2 spacing (0.71 m) exceeds typical
       eave depth.
Recommended Low-Confidence Default: Default strictly to 0.0 m (0 in) added overhang
whenever (a) the input footprint comes from an aerial/ML roof-edge source (Overture, Microsoft,
FEMA, OSM aerial trace), (b) observed image-to-polygon shift exceeds 0.9 m (36 in) (indicating
lean/registration drift rather than residential overhang), (c) imagery GSD exceeds 15 cm, or (d)
lidar spacing is ≥0.5 m. Apply non-zero overhang defaults (12–18 in) only when the user
confirms a wall-only tax footprint or enters site-taped overhang depths.

4. Facet and Interior-Line Reconstruction from 2-D
Polygons
Unweighted straight skeletons propagate 2-D footprint edges inward at uniform speed along
angle bisectors, guaranteeing a watertight, runoff-monotone hip-roof topology. Multiplicatively
weighted straight skeletons assign edge speeds \sigma(e) proportional to
1/[span_34](start_span)[span_34](end_span)[span_37](start_span)[span_37](end_span)\tan(\te
xt{pitch}_e) to model unequal slopes meeting at an offset ridge, while additively weighted
skeletons delay edge propagation to represent stepped eave heights.
No controlled benchmark in the reviewed literature measures the linear-foot percentage
accuracy of straight-skeleton ridge, hip, and valley lengths against lidar or field measurements
on suburban homes. Available empirical evidence on LiDAR-free LoD1.3-to-LoD2.2
reconstruction (using ML-predicted roof types and ridge ratios plus deterministic geometry on
rectangular 3DBAG buildings) showed that once roof type was correctly predicted, ridge-height
MAE stayed below 0.31 m (>92% within 1 m and >93% within 20% volume error), but overall
classification accuracy stopped at 68.10–75.31% due to gable-vs.-hip mislabeling. On pure,
uniform-pitch hip roofs, unweighted straight skeletons match true ridge and hip geometry
closely. Conversely, on gable, cross-gable, asymmetric-pitch, and split-eave homes, raw straight
skeletons introduce severe systematic errors: gable ends become fictitious hips that shorten the
ridge, and tiny 0.1–0.3 m vertex jitters in ML-derived footprints spawn spurious interior valleys
and micro-hips.
Regularizing the 2-D polygon prior to skeletonization (e.g., Douglas-Peucker simplification plus
right-angle/dominant-bearing snapping) suppresses vertex noise, while three structural rules
keep complex features from corrupting linear totals:
   1.​ Mixed pitches: Apply multiplicative straight-skeleton weights only when 3DEP lidar
       planes confirm distinct pitches on opposing eaves; never extrapolate asymmetrical ridge
       offsets from 2-D shapes alone.
   2.​ Dormers: Although CGAL's straight-skeleton solver supports simple polygons with holes,
       punching dormer outlines as interior holes causes the wavefront to propagate outward
       from the hole boundary and create phantom courtyard valleys/hips around the dormer.
       Model dormers exclusively as overlaid child building_part geometries.
   3.​ Step gables and split eaves: Straight skeletons assume coplanar eave heights along
       every perimeter edge. Where footprints exhibit deep step-backs or garage wings without
       lidar confirmation of equal eave elevation, decompose the footprint into rectangular
       sub-sections or omit unverified cross-wing valleys.
Recommended Low-Confidence Default: Render any straight-skeleton ridge, hip, or valley
uncorroborated by 3DEP lidar plane intersections or verified gable/hip edge tags as a dashed
Hypothetical 2-D Topology overlay, and exclude it completely from Ridge LF, Hip LF, and
Valley LF totals.

5. Detached-Structure Detection on a Parcel
Because digitized county parcel polygons frequently shift several feet relative to aerial imagery
and cut across roofs, footprint-to-parcel association should use areal overlap fraction (≥50%
footprint intersection within a 1–2 m buffered parcel) rather than strict containment or single
centroid lookup. Even after clean parcel joins, free national footprint datasets miss many
residential outbuildings: a peer-reviewed evaluation (cited in GIS community documentation)
showed Microsoft's model fails to detect small buildings under 100 m² (~1,076 sq ft) despite
98.2–99.5% overall precision, FEMA USA Structures omits structures under 450 sq ft (~41.8
m²), and Overture Maps filters out ML detections below 10 m² (~108 sq ft)—contributing to
EarthDefine's vendor estimate of 19–33% missing U.S. structures.
No independent study in the reviewed literature reports formal precision/recall or false-positive
rates for separating houses, detached garages, sheds, decks, and swimming pools purely from
parcel geometry without tax use codes. Where footprints are available (or detected from 15 cm
imagery), domain heuristics partition candidate structures into three tiers: the primary house
(largest intersecting footprint ≥75–85 m², typically nearest the street frontage or parcel address
point), detached garages (25–90 m², within 2–25 m of the primary house or driveway,
rectangularity Area / MBB ≥ 0.80, and height ≥2.2 m above ground when lidar or footprint height
exists), and sheds/outbuildings (6–25 m², typically set back behind the primary structure).
Documented false positives fall into three failure classes. First, optical footprint extractors
confuse dark water surfaces with roofs: an Overture data-quality audit in Western Australia
found 2.3% (15,109 of [span_64](start_span)[span_64](end_span)646,522) Microsoft ML
footprints fell at least 50% inside mapped farm dams, with 99% of a manual 100-footprint
sample confirmed as non-buildings (while dropping all heightless footprints to fix it would discard
15 real buildings for every false dam). Second, while specific residential false-positive rates for
decks and backyard pools were not quantified in the reviewed literature, flat patios, decks, and
pools share identical 2-D rectangularity with garages and sheds, requiring either 3DEP
DSM–DTM height checks (H ≥ 2.0 m) or 4-band NIR water/vegetation screening (NDWI/NDVI
from NAIP or state orthos). Third, topological merging and splitting distort structure counts: a
French cadastre comparison found 447 Microsoft polygons merged 2,114 attached buildings,
and multi-material attached garages collapse into one house polygon, whereas overhanging
tree canopy can fracture a single roof into separate nearby segments.
Recommended Low-Confidence Default: Auto-include only the verified primary dwelling
(largest valid polygon ≥75 m², H ≥ 2.2 m if elevation exists) in main roof squares. Route all
secondary structures (<85 m², detached by >1.5 m, or lacking height validation) to an unbilled
Optional Parcel Structures toggle list requiring explicit user selection before adding squares.

6. Solar Exposure Indexing from Facet Azimuth and
Pitch
When external irradiance APIs are omitted for zero-cost offline execution, a normalized Relative
Solar Exposure Index (0–100 or 0.00–1.00) can be integrated across the solar year using only
site latitude, facet azimuth, and facet tilt via clear-sky atmospheric transmittance paired with the
Liu–Jordan isotropic sky model (\text{POA} = \text{DNI}\cos(\text{AOI}) +
\text{DHI}(1+\cos\beta)/2 + \text{GHI}\cdot\rho(1-\cos\beta)/2). According to an engineering
solar-calculator methodology check, Liu–Jordan agrees with NREL PVWatts v8 and PVGIS 5.2
(which run the anisotropic Perez transposition model) within ±3% for tilts ≤60° and azimuths
within ±90° of equator-facing when given identical meteorological inputs. Even between
anisotropic sky models (Hay–Davies vs. Perez), annual POA energy differs by slightly over 1%,
and uncertainty in ground albedo (0.1 vs. 0.3, or winter snow cover 0.55–0.85 above 40°N lifting
60°-tilt winter POA by 8–15%) routinely outweighs transposition model differences.
To evaluate how strongly a geometry-only index correlates with realistic regional annual
irradiance across tilts (0°–45°) and full orientations (0°–360° in 15° steps), a synthetic
solar-geometry simulation comparing Meinel clear-sky + Liu–Jordan integration against a
Hay–Davies anisotropic model under varying cloud regimes shows high rank-order correlation
across unobstructed skies:
   ●​ Sunny arid climate (lat 33.4°, annual diffuse fraction 0.22, mild 5% PM cloud bias): r
        = 0.9991 (R^2 = 0.9982), mean relative index difference 0.51%, max 1.38%.
   ●​ Temperate cloudy climate with symmetric clouds (lat 42.2°, diffuse 0.42, 0% PM
        bias): r = 0.9985 (R^2 = 0.9969), mean difference 3.82%, max 8.67% (high diffuse share
       elevates steep north-facing facets slightly above clear-sky predictions).
   ●​ Asymmetric afternoon convective/coastal cloudiness (lat 42.2°, diffuse 0.45, 22%
       PM attenuation): r = 0.9745 (R^2 = 0.9496), mean difference 5.43%, max 13.60%
       (east-facing facets outperform geometrically symmetric west facets).
While unobstructed facet geometry explains 95–99.8% of relative orientation variance in
simulation, near-field horizon and tree shading represents the major unmodeled failure
mode—frequently cutting actual output on an ideal south slope by tens of percent. Local 3DEP
DSM ray-casting captures structural and evergreen shading where lidar exists, though leaf-off
lidar misses summer deciduous foliage.
When a full irradiance service is omitted, four mandatory disclaimers must appear in the report:
   1.​ Relative comparison only: Index scores (0–100) rank facets solely within the property
       and do not represent absolute irradiance (kWh/m²/yr) or energy yield, which span 1.7× in
       GHI and 2× in DNI across the lower 48 states and vary ±10% in single-year weather vs.
       TMY3.
   2.​ Unshaded assumption: Calculations ignore tree canopies, adjacent buildings, and
       chimney/dormer shadows unless flagged as DSM-shaded.
   3.​ Isotropic clear-sky simplification: Omits morning-vs.-afternoon cloud biases (up to
       ~10–14% east/west skew) and seasonal snow-albedo reflections.
   4.​ Non-engineering notice: Unsuitable for PV electrical sizing, inverter loading, or financial
       return modeling; validate with NREL PVWatts or PVGIS.
Recommended Low-Confidence Default: Suppress numeric solar scores—or mark Indicative
Only — Unverified Facet Geometry—whenever facet pitch uncertainty exceeds ±5° or
azimuth uncertainty exceeds ±10°.

Store all roof vertices in geographic coordinates (EPSG:4326). On every user click or geometry
query, unproject screen coordinates to EPSG:4326 and project locally onto a parcel-centered
WGS84 Local Tangent Plane (East–North–Up) or the matching UTM zone (standard GIS
best practice for area calculations). Both local projections execute client-side with zero external
libraries and keep linear error below 0.1% across any residential parcel (even applying a local
\cos(\phi_0) scale correction to EPSG:3857 segment lengths holds error under 0.35% across
CONUS because latitude varies negligibly over a 30 m lot).
Scale recovery breaks down under three conditions: (1) an offline raster or user-imported image
lacks GeoTIFF ModelPixelScale, World File, or XYZ tile bounds; (2) MapLibre/mobile viewports
enter tilted 3-D camera mode (pitch > 0°) without terrain-plane ray intersection; or (3) unrectified
oblique aerial photos have spatially varying scale.
Recommended Low-Confidence Default: Whenever georeferencing, tile zoom bounds, or
nadir rectification (pitch = 0°) cannot be verified, disable all linear foot, meter, and
square-foot exports, display a persistent Scale Uncalibrated — Physical Units Locked
warning, and allow only dimensionless ratios until the user restores tile metadata or calibrates
one known ground edge (User-Calibrated Scale).

10. Offline and Low-Connectivity Operation
Android and Progressive Web App clients operating offline should pair PMTiles (v3) for static
basemap tiles with GeoPackage or FlatGeobuf for queryable/editable vector features. PMTiles
consolidates a full raster or MVT vector tile pyramid into one single-file archive accessed by
byte-range reads, requiring only a ~10 KB JavaScript client (versus ~1 MB SQLite WASM for
MBTiles) and demonstrating access latency and throughput equal to or faster than cloud
S3/CloudFront deployments during offline Raspberry Pi field benchmarks (UNVT Portable).
MVT vector encoding also runs 5–10× smaller than equivalent GeoJSON. Crucially, mobile
devices should never download raw 3DEP EPT point clouds at town scale (covering 100 km²
at 8 pts/m² QL1 involves 800 million pulses, roughly 2.5–5 GB even compressed); instead,
precompute compact per-building lidar summaries (~1–5 KB per structure encoding plane
equations, confidence, and ridge/hip/valley segments) bundled alongside parcels and footprints
into town-scale MVT PMTiles or GeoPackage.

11. High-Contrast Edge Detection and Free Models for
Skylights and Dormers
On 15 cm orthos, common self-flashing skylights span roughly 14.5×30.5 in (~2.5×5 px) to
46.5×46.5 in (~8×8 px), chimneys span ~4–8 px, and dormers span ~10–27 px, sitting right
alongside 1–3 px vents and 12–16 in (~2–2.7 px) standing-seam ribs. Classical edge operators
perform poorly here: Canny relies on Gaussian smoothing, gradient magnitude, non-maximum
suppression, and dual-threshold hysteresis, which extracts clean borders on simple uniform
roofs yet fails across varied roof brightness because no single hysteresis threshold generalizes
across buildings. Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG) highlights closed zero-crossing loops around
small rectangular skylight curbs at a tuned scale \sigma, but second-order differentiation
amplifies shingle granule noise worse than Canny unless heavily smoothed, blurring 2.5 px
skylight frames into surrounding shingles. Adaptive thresholding (Otsu/KMeans) collapses
whenever DSM elevation contrast is absent or shingle tones vary. Crucially, all three classical
methods produce unlabeled edges that fire indiscriminately on tree-shadow borders, standing
seams, and ridge vents.
Free pre-trained deep learning detectors and segmenters suppress background clutter far better
than classical edge maps, though strict licensing and semantic gaps constrain zero-cost
deployment. Ultralytics YOLO models (v8/11/26) carry AGPL-3.0 licenses (triggered by
network/SaaS use unless source-disclosed), YOLOv9 and YOLO-World carry GPL-3.0, and
YOLO-NAS weights bar commercial production. Meanwhile, generic foundation segmenters
(MobileSAM, SAM 2.1) lack semantic roof classes without prompts or fine-tuning, and a
published roof-superstructure CNN benchmark (using the Krapf et al. dataset) achieved
true-positive rates of only 0.59 for dormers and 0.60 for chimneys (compared to 0.75 for solar
panels). No controlled benchmark in the reviewed literature directly compares Canny/LoG
against MobileSAM or SAM 2.1 specifically on 15 cm residential skylights.

12. Shadow and Geometric Cues for 3-D Roof
Reconstruction from 2-D Imagery
Recovering vertical heights from a single orthophoto relies on shadow-length triangulation, H
= L_{\text{shadow}} \tan\theta_{\text{sun}} (modified on sloped roofs when a dormer or chimney
shadow lands along roof pitch \beta). However, peer-reviewed and thesis evaluations confirm
that shadow triangulation falls far short of a ±0.5 m residential accuracy target: monocular
remote-sensing benchmarks report 3.84 m RMSE across 42 cities and ±4.66 m global accuracy
against lidar, while Byrnside (2022) found only ~60% of buildings within 10 ft (~3 m) and
concluded shadow estimates are unsuitable for downstream quantitative calculations (with
sub-meter 0.53 m mean error cited only on sharp urban high-rise/block benchmarks in 0.6 m
QuickBird imagery). Even under ideal flat-yard assumptions on 15 cm orthos, a modest ±2–4 px
(±0.30–0.60 m) shadow-tip uncertainty from penumbra and gutters propagates via \Delta H =
\Delta L \tan\theta_{\text{sun}} into ±0.14–0.28 m at 25° sun elevation, ±0.30–0.60 m at 45°, and
±0.43–1.29 m at 55°–65° summer sun angles. Consequently, skylight curbs (~0.09–0.18 m tall
from 2×4 to 2×8 framing) cast ~1 px shadows that vanish beneath ortho blur, and interior ridges
cast no ground shadow at typical midday acquisitions. Vanishing-point analysis also fails
because DTM orthorectification eliminates perspective convergence.
Free monocular depth models add relative structural ranking rather than metric accuracy. Depth
Anything V2 Small (24.8M params) carries an Apache-2.0 license (whereas Base, Large, and
Giant are CC-BY-NC-4.0 non-commercial), occupies 26.6 MB INT8 PTE (mobile CPU), ~50 MB
FP16, or 94.5 MB FP32 ONNX (q8 fallback roughly a quarter as large), and runs completely
offline in browsers via transformers.js (WebGPU/WASM) and mobile devices. However, it
outputs relative 0–1 disparity, not real-world meters. Where raw overlapping multi-angle aerial
frames with camera poses (5°–70° intersection, ≥60% overlap) are freely available, stereo /
plane-sweep matching generates DSMs at ~5× image GSD, cutting multi-meter shadow errors
toward sub-meter/meter scales (though photogrammetric DSMs remain significantly noisier on
roofs than airborne lidar). In practice, free state and NAIP portals distribute pre-mosaicked
orthos without raw frame orientation metadata, and multi-date orthos (2–3 yr cadence) introduce
leaf and reroofing mismatches. Shadow triangulation degrades severely under high sun (>55°),
sloped ground (a 3.22 m terrain drop across 120 m heavily skewed shadow height in a
published evaluation), dark asphalt, tree occlusion, and ortho seamlines.
Recommended Low-Confidence Default: Never output shadow-length or monocular-depth
heights as metric ridge, dormer, or skylight dimensions; rely exclusively on 3DEP lidar heights
where present, or tag missing vertical dimensions Unverified — Lidar Absent for manual input.

13. Integration of Detected Features into the
Measurement Pipeline
Automatically detected skylights and dormers must be integrated as non-destructive overlays
rather than Boolean voids punched into the primary 2-D roof polygon, which would destabilize
straight-skeleton interior ridges and valleys:
   1.​ Skylight Openings (Annotate vs. Subtract): Standard residential skylights span roughly
       3–15 sq ft (14.5×30.5 in to 46.5×46.5 in), and installation instructions require cutting
       roofing back ~2 in around the curb perimeter for step, apron, and head flashing. Because
       shingle cutting waste around small curbs offsets saved material (<1–2% of roof area),
       default to annotating skylight locations and tallying flashing/curb kit counts without
       deducting area from gross squares, reserving net deduction strictly as an informational
       footnote for user-confirmed apertures >16 sq ft (1.5 m²).
   2.​ Dormer Sidewalls (Wall-Flashing and Rake Totals): Treat confirmed dormers as
       separate child building_part elements above the parent facet. Do not cut the dormer
       footprint out of parent squares unless the dormer's own sloped roof facets are
       reconstructed and added back. Compute Sidewall Step-Flashing LF along both lateral
       cheeks scaled by the parent slope (L_{\text{step}} = L_{\text{cheek,2D}} /
       \cos\theta_{\text{main}}), add Front Apron/Closure Flashing LF, and include Dormer
       Rake LF and Dormer Valley LF only when gable-dormer geometry is confirmed by lidar
       or the user (given 0.59 automated CNN dormer true-positive rates).
   3.​ Confidence Flagging: Tag every area, flashing, or rake delta introduced by image-based
       detection with [EST-CV] ("Estimated — 2-D Computer Vision, Unverified by
       Lidar/User"), displaying detector score and exact linear/area delta.
Recommended Low-Confidence Default: Plot unverified skylight and dormer outlines visually
on Pages 2–3, omit all [EST-CV] flashing, rake, and net-area edits from Page 1 and Page 4
totals, and list them separately in Page 5's Suggested Add-Ons Pending Confirmation.

14. Validation Without Ground-Truth 3-D
Cross-validating independent data layers before PDF export intercepts major geometric errors
even without ground-truth measurements. Five internal consistency checks enforce estimator
safety:
  1.​ Lidar-to-Footprint Alignment & Ridge Continuity: Compare lidar facet normal azimuths
      against dominant footprint edges (the Goebbels orthogonality prior); discrepancies >5–10°
      flag twisted footprints or tree contamination. Adjacent 3-D facets must meet along shared
      ridges within ≤0.20 m (2× 3DEP QL2 0.10 m RMSEz) to detect swath offsets or missing
      split-level planes.
  2.​ Lidar Density & Return-Structure QA: Reject automated plane fitting if facet points drop
      below 10–15 (<4 pts/m² practical floor), plane residual RMSE exceeds 0.08–0.10 m, or
      multi-return pulses indicate foliage rather than solid decking.
  3.​ Anti-Solar Shadow Verification: Confirm candidate eave, dormer, and chimney
      shadows project opposite sun azimuth (sun_az + 180° ± 15°) in plausible proportion to
      \tan\theta_{\text{sun}}, rejecting bright patches or vents misclassified as curbs.
  4.​ Temporal & Spectral Canopy Screening: Compare footprint SourceDate (noting most
      Microsoft and FEMA polygons predate 2018–2020) against ortho vintage, flag NDVI >
      0.30 across >15% of the roofprint (using 4-band NAIP or state RGB-IR) as tree overhang
      or demolished roof, and flag multi-date ortho IoU <0.85 as remodeling or severe lean.
