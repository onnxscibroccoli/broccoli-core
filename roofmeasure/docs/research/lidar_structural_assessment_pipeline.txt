End-to-End High-Density LiDAR
Structural Assessment Production
Pipeline
Achieving sub-centimeter (<10 mm) structural boundary accuracy in an automated LiDAR
production pipeline requires coupling close-range, high-density acquisition with multi-point
geometric primitive intersection rather than direct point-edge picking. Analytical error
propagation across sensor platforms indicates that single-point boundary localization is
physically capped by spatial discretization and mixed-edge beam footprint effects—modeled at
7.6 mm on terrestrial laser scanners (TLS) at 25 m, 27.5–46.1 mm on unmanned aerial vehicle
(UAV) LiDAR at 60 m, and 137.3–245.9 mm on airborne laser scanning (ALS) at 800–1,200 m.
Intersecting fitted planar facets suppresses random ranging, attitude, and footprint variance
inversely with the square root of facet point support, driving modeled relative ridge, valley, and
intersection-line uncertainty down to 0.45 mm for TLS, 2.41 mm for mobile laser scanning
(MLS), and 4.53–7.66 mm for UAV LiDAR (while absolute georeferencing remains bounded by
trajectory accuracy). Public regional ALS datasets such as USGS 3D Elevation Program (3DEP)
Quality Level 1 (QL1) and Quality Level 2 (QL2)—specified at ≤10 cm non-vegetated vertical
RMSE, ≤4–8 cm swath-overlap RMSD, and ≥2–8 pulses/m²—cannot meet sub-centimeter
boundary tolerances on typical roof facets because multi-decimeter laser footprints and
multi-centimeter trajectory drift dominate the error budget.
Operationalizing this precision across planar, curved, glazed, and open-decked structures
requires a six-stage computational architecture: (1) Cloud Optimized Point Cloud (COPC) octree
ingestion paired with post-processed GNSS-INS trajectory files (SBET) to recover exact
per-pulse range and ray geometry; (2) four-stage radiometric calibration via the extended-target
LiDAR range equation accounting for sensor gain, near-field overlap, spherical spreading,
two-way Kruse/Kim atmospheric attenuation, and non-Lambertian Oren–Nayar incidence
effects; (3) multi-scale octree voxelization, Weighted RANSAC, and constrained region-growing
assembled into closed 2-manifold Boundary Representations (B-Reps) via Kinetic Shape
Reconstruction (KSR) or integer programming; (4) Moving Least Squares (APSS/RIMLS)
implicit modeling, Weingarten principal curvature classification, First Fundamental Form
Gauss–Legendre area integration, and discrete developable unrolling (ABF++/LSCM) for curved
shells; (5) hybrid semantic classification for dormers, silicate glazing, and penetrable decking;
and (6) joint manifold topology optimization and multi-pass transient ray-carving to purge
specular reflection ghosts.
Two empirical evidence gaps constrain full automation and require calibration gates rather than
fixed universal constants. First, while aerial-imagery deep learning yields a 0.59 true-positive
rate on dormers and rolling-window LiDAR range variance detects frameless glass at 96.2%
mean accuracy, no independent benchmark in the reviewed studies reports precision/recall for
pure LiDAR intersection-based dormer extraction, universal multi-return porosity thresholds
separating open-slat decking from solid flooring, or roof-specific false-positive elimination rates
for multi-pass transient specular rejection. Second, an audit of USGS 3DEP repositories found
that 63% of 2,529 sampled LAS files populated the system identifier field with software or
vendor names rather than the physical instrument; whenever sensor metadata or Automatic
Gain Control (AGC) logs are missing, absolute reflectance inversion must fall back to empirical
swath-overlap normalization.

Sensor Feasibility and Sub-Centimeter Boundary
Error Budget
Certifying sub-centimeter boundaries begins by decomposing the single-point error budget
across five uncorrelated contributors: trajectory positioning (\sigma_{\text{traj}}),
attitude/boresight uncertainty scaled by range (R \sigma_{\text{att}}), range precision
(\sigma_{\text{range}}), mixed-edge footprint ambiguity where a finite laser spot (d_{\text{spot}}
= d_0 + R \beta_t for exit aperture d_0 and divergence \beta_t) straddles a fascia or parapet
(\approx 0.25 d_{\text{spot}}), and sampling discretization (\text{spacing}/\sqrt{12}). Direct edge
picking fails even at high UAV densities because footprint averaging and discrete point spacing
create decimeter-to-centimeter jitter.
Modeling structural edges as the line of intersection between two fitted planar facets of N points
meeting at dihedral angle \Delta\alpha reduces random perpendicular uncertainty proportionally
to \sqrt{2}\sigma_{\text{rand}} / (\sqrt{N/3}\sin\Delta\alpha). Evaluating this analytical
propagation model—parameterized with a 3 mm exit aperture, 20 m² facets meeting at a 53.13°
dihedral angle (two 6/12 slopes), and a 0.30 intra-pass drift factor for relative
dimensioning—separates platforms viable for sub-centimeter dimensioning from regional
screening datasets.

First, boresight misalignment and swath-to-swath trajectory drift act as correlated systematic
shifts that do not average down with point count N; strip-adjustment bundle calibration is
mandatory before facet extraction to hold intraswath repeatability near the 1.2 cm
smooth-surface precision benchmarked on calibrated UAV LiDAR sites and well inside USGS
QL0 limits (≤3 cm repeatability, ≤4 cm overlap RMSD). Second, airborne data at 16 pts/m² still
exhibits 25 cm spot spacing, whereas TLS-grade registration references are typically acquired
at ≥1,000 pts/m² and achieve 1.7–3.6 cm RMSE when benchmarked against amorphous objects
and three-plane corners. Third, public USGS 3DEP holdings—accessible via anonymous
s3://usgs-lidar-public/ Entwine Point Tiles (EPT) or Requester Pays s3://usgs-lidar/ raw LAZ 1.4
buckets—serve strictly as macro context and regional terrain baselines rather than
sub-centimeter CAD sources.

Stage 1: Cloud-Native LAS/LAZ Octree Ingestion and
Trajectory Synchronization
Production ingestion standardizes raw LAS 1.4, LAZ, and EPT archives into Cloud Optimized
Point Cloud (COPC 1.0) containers coupled with post-processed trajectory telemetry. A COPC
file is a backwards-compatible, variably chunked LAZ 1.4 file embedding a clustered octree
hierarchy inside Variable Length Records (VLRs) and Extended VLRs (EVLRs). Strict schema
normalization is enforced at ingest: COPC requires LAS 1.4 Point Data Record Formats
(PDRFs) 6, 7, or 8—supporting 256 classification values, extended return numbering, GPS
timestamps, and optional RGB (PDRF 7) or RGB plus near-infrared (PDRF 8). Older LAS 1.2
files (PDRFs 0–5) must be upgraded explicitly using untwine, PDAL writers.copc, or
copc-converter with valid Coordinate Reference System (CRS) definitions and classification-flag
validation.
Deterministic out-of-core HTTP byte-range streaming proceeds through four sequential
operations:
    1.​ Header and Info VLR Prefetch: Because the 160-byte copc-info VLR is fixed as the first
        VLR starting at byte offset 375 immediately following the LAS 1.4 header, workers issue a
        single 0–535 byte HTTP Range request to retrieve global bounding coordinates, octree
        spacing, and the root hierarchy page pointer.
    2.​ In-Memory Octree Traversal: Workers fetch the root hierarchy page and walk the
        clustered octree—where level 1 divides the bounding box into 8 octants and level 2 into
        64 sub-cubes, typically extending to levels 6–8 for urban datasets—culling nodes outside
        parcel bounds or below the requested Level-of-Detail resolution parameter.
    3.​ Chunk Decoding and Local Staging: Surviving octree nodes map directly to the LAZ
        chunk table for parallel range fetching (typically capped near PDAL's default of 15
        concurrent HTTP request threads so network I/O does not outpace decompression and
        inflate memory). While COPC dramatically cuts selective transfer costs—reading 4.1 MB
        in five requests for a 100 m square at medium resolution from a 2.6 GB file, or retrieving
        41 thousand, 620 thousand, and 4.1 million points across 10 m, 2 m, and 0.5 m
        resolutions over 1 km²—FOSS4G-NA 2026 benchmarks comparing LAS versus COPC
        report that once files are cached locally or query windows are large, local LAS
        outperforms remote COPC. Production workers therefore leverage COPC (which shrinks
        raw LAS by up to 4×) for discovery and bounding-box triage, then stage target building
        clips in local NVMe or memory before iterative geometric fitting.
    4.​ Trajectory Synchronization: Because COPC provides no ept-sources.json per-file
        metadata index, ingestion preserves PointSourceId (flight-line or scan-station ID) and
        GpsTime (optionally indexed via COPC's temporal EVLR specification) to link points with
        Applanix Smoothed Best Estimate of Trajectory (SBET) binary files. SBET files store
        post-processed GNSS-INS trajectories calibrated for lever arms and boresight alignment
        (reported by practitioners as unprojected WGS84 coordinates in radians). Interpolating
        instantaneous sensor position \mathbf{S}(t_i) at each point's GPS timestamp recovers
        exact slant range R_i = \Vert{}\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{S}(t_i)\Vert{}_2 and incident ray
        direction \mathbf{v}_i = (\mathbf{S}(t_i) -

Stage 2: Multi-Stage Radiometric Calibration via the
LiDAR Range Equation
Raw LiDAR intensity values conflate surface material properties with spherical spreading,
hardware gain, near-field beam geometry, atmospheric extinction, and surface orientation.
Under the extended-target LiDAR range equation—valid when the target facet exceeds the
laser footprint—received optical power P_r depends on transmitted power P_t, receiver aperture
diameter D_r, system optical transmission \eta_{\text{sys}}, near-field overlap function \eta(R),
one-way atmospheric transmission T(R) (entering squared for the two-way path), range R,
incidence angle \alpha, and target reflectance \rho:
P_r(R, \alpha, \rho) = \frac{P_t D_r^2}{4 R^2} \eta_{\text{sys}} \, \eta(R) \, T^2(R) \, \rho \,
f_{\text{BRDF}}(\alpha)
Following the Level 0 (raw digital number) to Level 1 (intensity correction) to Level 3 (absolute
radiometric calibration) framework, normalization proceeds across four stages.

Hardware Gain, Near-Field Overlap, and Spherical Range
Normalization
Sensors equipped with Automatic Gain Control (AGC) dynamically adjust amplifier gain G_c to
keep detector output near a nominal setpoint across large input signal swings (documented in
closed-loop lidar designs to maintain ±10% output stability across ~150% input variability). Level
1 models invert G_c explicitly where recorded; where proprietary AGC logs or hardware
identifiers are absent, the pipeline executes relative strip-overlap matching across overlapping
flight lines or terrestrial stations. Next, close-range terrestrial and mobile scans apply an
empirical near-field overlap curve \eta(R) below the scanner's full-crossover range—typically 6
m on short-range spinning units and leveling out above 15 m on close-range terrestrial
scanners—before multiplying extended-target intensities by R^2. Multi-return pulses caused by
beam splitting across roof edges or vegetation are flagged and adjusted using overlap-driven or
cumulative-energy normalization.

Absolute Reflectance Inversion
Level 3 radiometric calibration converts corrected intensity I_{\text{corr}} into absolute surface
reflectance \rho \in [0, 1] via empirical reference targets (Spectralon panels, calibrated tarps, or
homogeneous sand/gravel). Because physical receivers exhibit nonlinear dynamic compression
across range, logarithmic parametric fitting of the form \hat{\rho} =
\frac{1}{f_{\text{BRDF}}(\alpha)} \exp\big((I_{\text{corr}} - p_2(R))/p_1(R)\big) was demonstrated
in terrestrial scanner trials to hold residual reflectance deviation to 6.1% on a 905 nm scanner
(2–22 m, 0°–50° incidence), while capturing material spectral divergence between
wavelengths—where a nominal 0.40 reference target calibrated to 0.467 at 905 nm versus
0.528 at 1550 nm (5–90 m, 0°–72° incidence).

Stage 3: Multi-Scale Planar Segmentation and
Closed-Loop Watertight Topology
High-density point clouds cause single-scale planar region growing to fracture. Benchmark
evaluations comparing 4 pts/m² and 40 pts/m² datasets show that while local PCA eigenvalue
coplanarity succeeds at low density, shrinking neighborhoods at 40 pts/m² expose micro-relief
on roof tiles, corrugated metal, and vertical ranging noise, destabilizing point normals and
fragmenting planar patches. Conversely, unweighted RANSAC produces spurious bridging
planes across adjacent slopes sharing similar heights or normal orientations.
Production segmentation resolves both pathologies through a three-stage coarse-to-fine
workflow:
   1.​ Coarse Octree Voxelization and Seed Clustering: Partition the point cloud into an
       octree; fit a least-squares plane inside each voxel and recursively subdivide into eight
       sub-voxels wherever maximum point-to-plane residual d_{\max} exceeds threshold, down
       to a minimum leaf scale d_s calibrated to smooth micro-texture (e.g., 0.25–0.50 m,
       ensuring ≥10 points per seed cell over an assumed minimum structural plane area

       n)[span_168](start_span)[span_168](end_span)a_p = 1.0\text{ m}^2). Merge adjacent
       planar voxels via agglomerative hierarchical clustering ordered by minimum merging
       Mean Squared Error (MSE) until MSE exceeds
       \e[span_169](start_span)[span_169](end_span)psilon_{\text{merge}}.
   2.​ Weighted RANSAC and Footprint Alignment: Over residual complex junctions, replace
       unweighted inlier counting with Weighted RANSAC—down-weighting inliers continuously
       by both perpendicular residual d_i and normal angular deviation \theta_i to suppress
       spurious cross-slope planes—or MLESAC log-likelihood maximization. Accelerated

       speedups over standard RANSAC and up to 4× over standard Randomized RANSAC with

       orthogonally (or at 45° for hips) to dominant footprint edge vectors further suppresses

Closed-Loop Watertight Topology and Edge Classification
Transforming disconnected planar patches into a closed-loop, watertight 3D B-Rep solid relies
on global arrangement optimization rather than local polygon stitching. Three production
formulations govern the trade-off between architectural regularity and primitive scalability.
PolyFit intersects detected planes (plus vertical contour discontinuity planes) into candidate
polygonal faces and selects a closed manifold subset via Mixed-Integer Linear Programming
(MILP) balancing data fitting, surface coverage, and model simplicity, achieving 0.27 m RMSE
on benchmark reconstructions; however, combinatorial complexity makes PolyFit
computationally prohibitive above ~100 planar primitives. Kinetic Shape Reconstruction
(KSR) extracts 2D boundary segments via alpha-shapes, grows polygonal faces kinetically
through collision state transitions to partition 3D space into polyhedral cells, and extracts a
strictly watertight, intersection-free mesh via graph min-cut inside/outside labeling—scaling
smoothly past PolyFit's 100-plane ceiling. SimpliCity takes a footprint-constrained route,
building a 2D polygonal partition regularized for orthogonality, parallelism, and collinearity before
extruding cells to 3D under global vertex-z planarity optimization.
Every edge in the closed B-Rep is labeled deterministically from incident face geometry. Interior
edges shared by two upward-facing roof planes whose normals diverge away from the
intersection line (convex exterior dihedral angle <180°) represent ridges when near-horizontal
(\vert{}\Delta z / L_{\text{horiz}}\vert{} \le 1^\circ) and hips when sloped toward eaves. Interior
edges where adjacent roof planes slope inward toward the seam (concave exterior dihedral
angle >180°) define valleys. Perimeter boundary edges separating a roof plane from a vertical
fascia/wall discontinuity or overhang drop are eaves when level with the ground (carrying
gutters and drip edge) and rakes (verge/barge edges) when sloped from eave to ridge along
gable end walls.
Critically, structural takeoffs must trace the physical roof drip edge rather than wall lines or
external 2D footprint polygons. An illustrative roofing calculation shows that on a 56 × 28 ft
ranch house with 18 in eave overhangs and 12 in rake overhangs, plan area expands from
1,568 sq ft at the walls to 1,798 sq ft at the roof edge (+14.7%), adding 249 sq ft (~2.5 roofing
squares) of sloped surface at a 5/12 pitch and 10.5 ft of drip edge. Relying on third-party ML
building footprints introduces substantial structural error: a commercial vendor evaluation by
EarthDefine claims Microsoft US Building Footprints (129.6M), FEMA USA Structures (135.3M),
and Overture Maps (158.4M) miss 33%, 30%, and 19% of structures relative to EarthDefine's
194.6M count; independent case testing in Vitré, France found Microsoft footprints matched only
25% of cadastral buildings at IoU > 0.5 (versus 83%–84% for OpenStreetMap/Overture)
because 447 Microsoft polygons merged 2,114 terraced buildings; peer-reviewed validation
confirmed Microsoft omits small structures (<100 m²) despite 98.2%–99.5% precision on large
buildings in Denver, New York, and Los Angeles; and an audit in Western Australia found
~15,000 Microsoft footprints sitting on farm dams (99% false positives in a 100-polygon manual
sample). Furthermore, imagery-derived footprints rectified only to bare-earth DTMs suffer radial
building lean—reaching 1.87–5.77 m of horizontal offset for 4–10 m tall buildings viewed
25°–30° off-nadir. LiDAR B-Reps extract the true 3D eave/rake outline directly and recover
setback wall lines via vertical facade intersection where terrestrial/oblique scans exist.

Stage 4: Differential Geometry and Isometric Unrolling
for Curved Roofs
Curved architectural shells—barrel vaults, domes, conoids, and hyperbolic
paraboloids—fracture into spurious facets under planar RANSAC and must route to a
continuous differential-geometry solver whenever plane-fitting residuals exceed d_t with
spatially coherent normal gradients.

Moving Least Squares Surface Projection and Bandwidth Selection
Curved clusters are projected onto a continuous implicit manifold via Moving Least Squares
(MLS). Algebraic Point Set Surfaces (APSS) fit local weighted algebraic spheres rather than
tangent planes, preserving circular/elliptic vault curvature, while Robust Implicit Moving Least
Squares (RIMLS) apply iterative reweighting on spatial and normal residuals to preserve sharp
C^0 crease edges where curved shells meet vertical parapets or standing-seam ribs. Kernel
support radius h is initialized at 2–3× the local point spacing and refined via Least-Squares
Cross-Validation (h_{\text{LSCV}}) minimizing asymptotic Integrated Squared Error
(\text{ISE}) so surface smoothing suppresses range jitter without flattening tight arches.

First Fundamental Form Area Integration
Computing curved surface areas from planar triangle meshes introduces systematic chordal
error. Parameterizing smooth MLS patches locally as \mathbf{r}(u, v) = (u, v, z(u, v)) yields E = 1
+ z_u^2, F = z_u z_v, and G = 1 + z_v^2, allowing exact surface area integration via 2D
composite 4-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature:
A = \iint_\Omega \sqrt{EG - F^2} \, du \, dv = \iint_\Omega \sqrt{1 + z_u^2 + z_v^2} \, du \, dv
Numerical benchmarking against analytical integrals confirms quadrature precision: on a
parabolic barrel vault (z = 3(1 - (u/6)^2) over [-6, 6] \times [0, 12] m; exact analytical area =
72(\sqrt{2} + \operatorname{arsinh}(1)) = 165.282275\text{ m}^2), a 16×16 4-point
Gauss–Legendre grid recovers 165.282275 m² (0.000000% error), whereas triangulated
meshes systematically underestimate area by -0.6452% at 4×4 (3.0 m step), -0.1607% at 8×8
(1.5 m), and -0.0401% at 16×16 (0.75 m). Conversely, on a hyperbolic paraboloid saddle (z =
2.5 uv / 36 over [-6, 6] \times [-6, 6] m; quadrature reference 152.024332 m²), 8×8 quadrature
matches to 0.000000%, while triangulated meshes overestimate area by +0.6180% at 4×4,
+0.1545% at 8×8, and +0.0386% at 16×16.

Numerical Isometric Unrolling for Developable Surfaces
Flattening 3D curved roofs into flat CNC sheet-metal or sheathing patterns without stretching or
tearing requires enforcing discrete developability. Zero vertex angle defect (\Omega_i = 2\pi -
\sum_{t \ni i} \theta_{i,t} = 0) guarantees local flattenability alone, not physical sheet-metal
bendability; true discrete developability simultaneously requires locally ruled hinge structure
where consecutive face normals in each vertex star collapse onto a curve on the Gauss sphere
(quantified by driving the smallest eigenvalue \lambda_i of the vertex-star normal covariance
matrix toward zero).
Unrolling executes in three sequential steps:
   1.​ Variational Developable Decomposition: Regions exhibiting |K| > \tau_K are partitioned
       along curvature-aligned seam curves via variational Gauss-image covariance energy
       minimization into piecewise developable islands.
   2.​ Isometric Ruling Unfolding: Strictly parabolic islands (K \approx 0) are unfolded
       sequentially across ruling edges by rigid 2D planar rotation, preserving exact edge
       lengths and surface areas.
   3.​ Quasi-Developable Parameterization: Where slight compound curvature remains, UV
       flattening uses Angle-Based Flattening (ABF++) rather than linear Least Squares
       Conformal Maps (LSCM). While LSCM solves discrete Cauchy–Riemann equations
       rapidly via linear algebra, it permits large peripheral area distortion and triangle foldovers
       on poor seams; ABF++ optimizes interior angles directly via Sequential Linearly
       Constrained Programming (SLCP), compressing the Hessian fivefold (up to 10× faster
       than original ABF) while guaranteeing bijective, flip-free flattening with low stretch.
       Edge-length strain and area ratios are logged per panel to flag any stretch exceeding
       material tolerances.

Stage 5: Hybrid Semantic Classification
Semantic parsing discriminates superstructures, transparent glazing, and porous decking from
solid structural roof planes by combining intersection geometry, Fresnel optics, and multi-return
penetration physics.

Intersection-Based Dormer Extraction
No independent benchmark in the reviewed literature reports precision or recall for pure LiDAR
geometric intersection-based dormer extraction, whereas 2D aerial-imagery convolutional
networks achieve only a 0.59 true-positive rate on dormers and 0.60 on chimneys (compared to
0.75 on solar modules). A three-step hybrid rule enforces geometric watertightness first:
  1.​ Host Facet Residual Isolation: Subtract every primary roof plane \pi_{\text{host}}
      identified in Stage 3 and isolate above-plane connected components whose horizontal
      hulls lie strictly interior to the host roof polygon.
  2.​ Sub-Facet Segmentation and Manifold Truncation: Apply fine-resolution
      RANSAC/region growing to extract the dormer's sub-roof facets and vertical cheek/front
      wall planes. Valid dormers must close via intersection valleys/apron seams onto
      \pi_{\text{host}}, with vertical walls extruded cleanly to the base polygon intersection line.
  3.​ Commercial-Safe Visual Cross-Check (Optional): When co-registered imagery is
      ingested, confirm window/trim context using Apache-2.0-licensed inference graphs free of
      AGPL-3.0 copyleft contamination—specifically MobileSAM (9.66M parameters, 10.5 MB
      quantized / 35 MB float ONNX), SAM 2.1 Hiera-Tiny (155.9 MB PyTorch checkpoint;
      ~148 MB encoder / ~15 MB decoder ONNX) [chap 70, 73, 77 -> cite: 70, 73, 77], or
      Baidu's RT-DETR from lyuwenyu/RT-DETR (avoiding Ultralytics' AGPL-3.0 wrapper).
      (While YOLOX code and weights carry Apache-2.0 terms, its repository has been inactive
      since mid-2025 with an unresolved March 2026 licensing thread on pretrained weights.)

Return Density Analysis for Penetrable Decking vs. Solid Flooring
Reviewed infrastructure inspection literature relies predominantly on Ground Penetrating Radar
(1.2–2.6 GHz) for concrete delamination and rebar mapping alongside macro LiDAR
bridge-deck displacement screening (ASPRS Class 17), leaving an explicit empirical gap: no
standardized benchmark in the reviewed studies establishes universal first-to-last return
ratio cutoffs distinguishing open-grating or spaced-plank decking from solid flooring.
Operationally, classification relies on physical pulse-footprint interaction rules calibrated to scan
geometry. On solid opaque floors (concrete, asphalt overlays, continuous plywood), pulses
terminate completely at the top surface (N_{\text{returns}} = 1, z_{\text{first}} \approx
z_{\text{last}}) with zero valid sub-surface returns directly underneath along the direct sightline.
On penetrable decking (steel grating or spaced planks), whenever beam spot diameter
d_{\text{spot}} straddles board edges, pulses split into multiple discrete returns
(N_{\text{returns}} \ge 2) while centered gap rays penetrate directly to sub-deck joists, water, or
ground (z_{\text{last}} < z_{\text{first}} - \Delta z_{\text{deck}}). Computing local sub-surface
transmission gap fraction P_{\text{gap}} = N_{\text{sub}} / N_{\text{ray}} jointly with
multi-return fraction and periodic intensity striation cleanly separates porous decking from solid
slabs whenever sub-deck clearance exceeds 3\sigma_{\text{range}} and is validated against the
survey's footprint-to-gap ratio.

Stage 6: Structural Topology Optimization and
Multi-Pass Transient Specular Rejection
Specular architectural reflectors—glazed skylights, curtain walls, standing-seam metal roofs,
and ponding water—deflect laser beams onto nearby parapets, chimneys, or walls, injecting
virtual "ghost" points behind the reflective plane. Experiments on modern interference-mitigated
LiDARs confirm that even a single planar mirror can induce up to 6.03 m of SLAM localization
drift and severe false-structure injection. Eliminating these artifacts combines exact reflection
kinematics, multi-pass visibility checking, and global manifold topology optimization.

Mirror Kinematics and Multi-Bounce Ray Unfolding
For every smooth planar candidate (glass or low-roughness metal) parameterized by centroid
\mathbf{C} and sensor-facing unit normal \mathbf{n} satisfying (\mathbf{S} - \mathbf{C}) \cdot
\mathbf{n} > 0, specular reflection behaves as if observed from a mirrored virtual sensor
\mathbf{S}_v, mapping any real diffuse obstacle \mathbf{p} onto a ghost coordinate
g(\mathbf{p}) behind the plane:
\mathbf{S}_v = \mathbf{S} - 2\big((\mathbf{S} - \mathbf{C}) \cdot \mathbf{n}\big)\mathbf{n},
\qquad g(\mathbf{p}) = \mathbf{p} - 2\big((\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{C}) \cdot
\mathbf{n}\big)\mathbf{n}
For any point \mathbf{p}_{\text{cand}} lying on the far side of a candidate reflector, the pipeline
evaluates the observation ray \mathbf{S} + u\mathbf{d} (u = 0 at sensor, u = 1 at
\mathbf{p}_{\text{cand}}) and checks whether 0 <
[span_286](start_span)[span_286](end_span)u \le 1 intersects inside the finite 3D polygon
boundary of the reflector. Because planar reflection is an involution (g(g(\mathbf{p})) =
\mathbf{p}), projecting \mathbf{p}_{\text{cand}} back across the mirror plane via
g(\mathbf{p}_{\text{cand}}) enables two-bounce ray tracing: whenever
g(\mathbf{p}_{\text{cand}}) coincides with a verified physical structure observed along
unobstructed direct sightlines, \mathbf{p}_{\text{cand}} is flagged as a specular ghost and
purged or unfolded back to its true object.

Multi-Pass Transient Consistency and Joint Manifold Optimization
No published benchmark in the reviewed studies quantifies standalone false-positive rejection
rates for multi-pass transient filtering on roofs; the workflow integrates multi-station mirror
tracking, physics-guided ray unfolding, and visibility carving across overlapping flight swaths or
TLS setups in three steps:
   1.​ Cross-Pass Pose-Invariance Filtering: Static diffuse structures remain fixed in world
       coordinates across all scanner poses \mathbf{S}_k, whereas reflections off slightly curved
       metal panels, wind-rippled ponding water, or misaligned glazing shift apparent positions
       \mathb[span_287](start_span)[span_287](end_span)[span_288](start_span)[span_288](e
       nd_span)f{p}_{\text{refl}}(\mathbf{S}_k) across passes indexed by PointSourceId.
       Clustering sub-surface points by pass identifies pose-dependent drift as transient
       multipath noise rather than fixed architecture.
   2.​ Cross-Pass Free-Space Ray Carving: Any candidate sub-surface voxel traversed
       cleanly by an unobstructed direct ray from an alternate scan station \mathbf{S}_{k'}
       terminating on a solid diffuse target beyond it violates free-space visibility and is pruned.
   3.​ Joint Manifold Topology Optimization: Remaining points pass to the global KSR graph
       min-cut or PolyFit MILP solver. Even if occluded single-sided reflections escape ray
       carving, isolated sub-surface ghost clusters lack closed watertight adjacency to the outer
       roof/wall shell, carry inverted visibility normals, and fail minimum support
       thresholds—preventing the global B-Rep from instantiating phantom faces in the final
       structural CAD export.
